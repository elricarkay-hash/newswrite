2. The ABCs of News Writing

Accuracy
The First Rule A story can be creative and compelling, but if it contains errors, it is worthless. Always check numbers, spelling of names, and basic facts. Accuracy always comes first.
Brevity
Get to the point Each word should do a job. If not, take it out. Don't be redundant. Say "8 a.m." — not "8 a.m. in the morning."
Clarity
Leave no questions Clarity starts with competent reporting. Understand your subject so completely that your story leaves it crystal-clear. Explain anything that wouldn't be obvious.

4. Writing The Lead & Variations

The Angle

The main idea of a news story and lead is called the "angle". It is the main point that the rest of the story will try to support.

Guidelines in Writing the Lead:

Pack the most important info in one sentence.
Start with the most important or unusual idea of the news event.
Go direct to the point.
Rarely use the "when" and "where" leads.
Use less than 30 words.
Avoid starting with articles: a, an, the.
Do not mention names in the lead unless the person is well-known.

Types of Leads:

Summary Lead
The classic approach. Tells the reader what the story is about instantly without destroying the flow of the sentence. Remember: use short, simple declarative sentences and do not cram everything into one sentence. Never use an important/unusual word twice.
"The city council unanimously approved a ₱500-million infrastructure budget Tuesday morning to rehabilitate the downtown drainage system before the rainy season begins."

5. The Pointers in Newswriting

  • One idea per sentence As much as possible, write one-idea, one-sentence paragraphs.
  • Limit sentence length Limit to not more than 25 words. Simplify. The simplest and clearest way of saying something is often the shortest and most eloquent.
  • Follow Subject-Verb-Object format Good news writing starts with good sentence structure. Refrain from using a lot of commas.
  • Limit prepositional phrases Use no more than three prepositional phrases per sentence.
  • Use strong verbs and active voice Write most of your sentences like this: "somebody does something". Note also that when a sentence is recast into active voice, it becomes shorter.
  • Reduce difficult words to simplest terms Use words understandable by the average reader. Explain technical terms briefly. Prefer concrete, specific words to abstract ones.
  • Choose the precise word
  • Keep it simple Write to express not to impress.
  • Be very careful Choose your words with care. Do not assume their meaning. Verify word meanings using high-quality references. Watch your grammar, spelling, capitalization, and punctuation.
  • Show, don't tell Don't just describe something. Reveal a piece of the world to yourself and to your readers.
  • Give your source when necessary People and documents need to be cited in attribution because that helps the reader evaluate the reliability of information.
  • Identify all persons Write their full names the first time they are mentioned.
  • Keep it objective Stay completely impartial. Do not use "I" and "me" unless you are quoting someone.
  • Quote people
  • Avoid preaching Do not preach at the end of the story.
  • Have an angle It can help make the purpose of the story clear and give it focus.
  • Read newspapers everyday Study constantly the construction of news stories in good national newspapers to improve your writing style and technique.

MANILA, Philippines – The House of Representatives on Wednesday, September 16, summoned Meta Platforms CEO Mark Zuckerberg to respond to concerns over child safety on its platforms.

The House also deferred action on the proposed P19.49-billion budget of the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and its attached agencies for 2027 pending resolution of issues involving Meta and online safety.

The DICT, in a social media post on Wednesday evening, said the issues with Meta were related to the Online Sexual Abuse and Exploitation of Children (OSAEC) and Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation Materials (CSAEM). These were seen as “grave threats” demanding action and accountability.

In addition to the OSAEC and CSAEM issues, further harms were also being facilitated or amplified on major platforms, including online illegal gambling, cyberbullying, social media addiction, depression, and related mental health risks that might endanger Filipinos, especially the young.

The DICT added that Meta Platforms “continues to fall short of its responsibility and commitment to youth safety.” The company’s scale and reach requires an obligation from them to set up proper safeguards, remove harmful content promptly, and cooperate with Philippine authorities.

The DICT, in its statement, said it would continue taking Meta to task for any missteps it makes, adding that “accountability cannot be optional.” “The safety and welfare of Filipino children must remain paramount. Technology companies that operate in the Philippines must be held to the highest standards of responsibility. Persistent failure to address these risks erodes public trust and cannot be tolerated,” it added.

In a separate DICT post early on Thursday morning, the House of Representatives said it summoned Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg to face the DICT to respond to its concerns surrounding harmful online content and platform accountability, alongside hopefully setting up measures to improve online safety for Philippine users.

Senator Robin Padilla on Thursday, Sept. 17, said he was overcome with emotion upon seeing former President Rodrigo Duterte appear in person before the International Criminal Court (ICC), but expressed sadness over the physical toll of his continued detention.

Padilla made the remarks a day after Duterte appeared at an ICC status hearing in The Hague, marking the former president’s first public appearance since his arrest in Manila and transfer to the tribunal’s custody in March 2025.

Asked for his reaction to Duterte’s appearance and observations that he appeared well enough to face trial, Padilla said seeing the former president again initially brought him joy.

“Ako po ay nabigla at hindi inaasahan na tumulo ang luha sa tuwa at nakita na siya (I was surprised and did not expect to shed tears of joy upon seeing him again),” Padilla said.

However, he said his happiness was tempered by concern over Duterte’s condition after spending more than a year in detention.

“Ngunit binalot ng lungkot dahil batid ko na malakas lang ang loob ni Tatay Digong. Kaya ng tapang at malasakit pero hindi na kaya ng 81-year-old niyang katawan ang kulungan (But it was also overshadowed by sadness because I know that Tatay Digong is only strong-willed. He has the courage and compassion, but his 81-year-old body can no longer endure imprisonment),” he added.

Duterte, 81, appeared at Wednesday’s hearing wearing a dark suit and white shirt and seated beside members of his defense team.

The status hearing focused on preparations for the presentation of the case, including discussions on prosecution witnesses and evidence and the amount of time the prosecution would need to present its case.

Duterte faces charges before the ICC over alleged crimes against humanity linked to his administration’s anti-drug campaign.

The former president has denied the allegations and previously maintained that the ICC has no jurisdiction over him.
