News Writing 101

1. The 7 Pillars of Newsworthiness

Timeliness

Satisfies the urgent need for the most current information; the first-mover advantage.

Proximity

Drives relevance through the physical or emotional nearness of the event to the reader.

Significance

Measures the magnitude of consequences; higher impact equals deeper engagement.

Prominence

Capitalizes on the public’s inherent interest in well-known individuals or institutions.

Oddity

Leverages the "shock value" of unique, bizarre, or extremely rare occurrences.

Conflict

Engages the reader through the inherent drama of opposing forces, ideas, or power dynamics.

Progress

Highlights communal advancement or positive movement, fostering civic interest.

2. Core Qualities Checklist

Accuracy
The First Rule Requires granular verification of numbers, spelling, and facts. A story can be stylistically brilliant, but a single factual error erodes institutional credibility.
Brevity
Word Economy Every word must justify its existence. Redundancy is the enemy of impact; a bloated narrative loses the reader before the core revelation.
Clarity
The Antidote The narrative must cut through official obfuscation. If the audience cannot comprehend the subject completely, the story fails as a democratic instrument.
Objectivity
Neutral Observer The journalist must remain protected and neutral. Introducing bias or "preaching" destroys standing and renders reportage a mere opinion piece.

4. Narrative Transformation

Hard News Structure

Concrete facts reported without elaboration. Follows the Lead-Succeeding-Ending hierarchy where importance descends.

Soft News Structure

Permits impressions and narration. Frequently utilizes reverse order, saving the most compelling primary information for the end to create a lasting impression.

6. Advanced Lead Construction

The Angle & Constraints

The main idea of a news story is the "angle". Lead constraints: Keep it under 30 words. Avoid starting with articles (A, An, The). Do not mention specific names unless widely recognized.

7. Syntax Command & Editorial Mandates

  • One idea per sentence: Strive for one-idea, one-sentence paragraphs to maximize clarity and pace.
  • The 25-word preference: While the lead has a 30-word ceiling, sentences in the body should generally not exceed 25 words.
  • Subject-Verb-Object (SVO): Use simple construction and refrain from excessive commas. The active voice is a mandate.
  • The Prepositional Limit: Use no more than three prepositional phrases per sentence to avoid clutter.
  • Length Ceiling: A paragraph should not exceed 75 words.
  • Sentence Count: If expanded beyond one sentence, do not exceed three sentences per paragraph.
  • The One-Sentence Standard: The one-paragraph sentence is preferred by editors for its "invisible architecture."
  • The Synthesis Ban: Do not use direct quotation and summary in the same paragraph. Ensure the source's voice is distinct from yours.
  • Impartiality: Stay completely objective. Do not use "I" or "me" unless directly quoting a source.
  • Avoid Preaching: Do not include a moralizing conclusion. Let the facts provide the message.
  • Show, Don't Tell: Do not merely describe; reveal the world through concrete details and strong verbs showing "somebody doing something."

MANILA, Philippines – The House of Representatives on Wednesday, September 16, summoned Meta Platforms CEO Mark Zuckerberg to respond to concerns over child safety on its platforms.

The House also deferred action on the proposed P19.49-billion budget of the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) and its attached agencies for 2027 pending resolution of issues involving Meta and online safety.

The DICT, in a social media post on Wednesday evening, said the issues with Meta were related to the Online Sexual Abuse and Exploitation of Children (OSAEC) and Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation Materials (CSAEM).

The DICT added that Meta Platforms “continues to fall short of its responsibility and commitment to youth safety.” The company’s scale and reach requires an obligation from them to set up proper safeguards, remove harmful content promptly, and cooperate with Philippine authorities.

In a separate DICT post early on Thursday morning, the House of Representatives said it summoned Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg to face the DICT to respond to its concerns surrounding harmful online content and platform accountability.

Instructor Analysis: The report utilizes a Summary Lead that packs the "Who" (House of Representatives), "When" (Wednesday), and "What" (summoned Mark Zuckerberg) into a single opening. The story demonstrates rigorous attribution by using the DICT as the primary source to validate concerns regarding child safety and budget deferment.

Sa ikatlong status conference ng International Criminal Court (ICC) Trial Chamber bago ang pagpapatuloy ng pagdinig sa kaso ni dating Pangulong Rodrigo Duterte, nagsagawa ng kilos-protesta ang ilang grupo ng mga Pilipinong manggagawa at naninirahan sa Netherlands nitong Miyerkoles (oras sa Pilipinas).

Panawagan nila sa mga Dutch at maging sa ICC, ituloy ang pagdetine kay Duterte.

Inihayag naman ng mga tagasuporta ng dating pangulo, na iilan lang ang nakapunta sa tapat ng ICC ngayong araw ng status conference. Ayon sa isa sa kanila, wala silang nakaplanong aktibidad ngayong araw, pero nakapasok siya at ilan pang kasama sa loob ng ICC para panoorin ang pagdinig.

Hindi pa isinasapubliko ng ICC Registry ang anumang dokumento patungkol sa maaaring waiver ng depensa, gayundin ang oposisyon dito ng prosekusyon at abogado ng mga drug war victim. Pero nang idaos na ang pagdinig, nakitang dumalo si Duterte.

Instructor Analysis: This coverage employs a Sequence Lead. By detailing the protest actions in the Netherlands before the hearing began, the writer builds tension. This chronological framing leads the reader to the major "Hard News" reveal: the appearance of the former president in the courtroom.

Senator Robin Padilla on Thursday, Sept. 17, said he was overcome with emotion upon seeing former President Rodrigo Duterte appear in person before the International Criminal Court (ICC).

Asked for his reaction to Duterte’s appearance and observations that he appeared well enough to face trial, Padilla said seeing the former president again initially brought him joy.

“Ako po ay nabigla at hindi inaasahan na tumulo ang luha sa tuwa at nakita na siya,” Padilla said.

However, he said his happiness was tempered by concern over Duterte’s condition. “Ngunit binalot ng lungkot dahil batid ko na malakas lang ang loob ni Tatay Digong... hindi na kaya ng 81-year-old niyang katawan ang kulungan,” he added.

The status hearing focused on preparations for the presentation of the case, including discussions on prosecution witnesses and evidence.

Duterte faces charges before the ICC over alleged crimes against humanity linked to his administration’s anti-drug campaign. The former president has denied the allegations.

Instructor Analysis: This piece illustrates the integration of "Soft News" (the Senator’s emotional response and "tears of joy") into a "Hard News" context (the ICC hearing). The writer balances the legal proceedings with the human interest of the physical toll of detention on an 81-year-old, providing a more compelling "Angle" for the reader.
